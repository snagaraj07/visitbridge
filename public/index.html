For parents of autistic kids

No more scary surprises at the doctor.

A check-up is full of surprises: strangers, loud rooms, a cuff that squeezes, something cold in their ear. VisitBridge makes an animated story of the exact visit, told out loud by a friend from your child's favorite thing. They watch it at home, so on the day, nothing is new.

Privacy and how it works

What stays on this device

Everything about your child: name, favorite thing, comfort item, needs, saved stories, and their pictures. They are kept in this browser only. There are no accounts. "Delete everything" removes them.

What is sent to the AI

To write the story: the visit type, the extra details you type, age range, favorite thing, comfort item, and the "hard" and "what helps" choices. Your child's name is not sent. The AI writes "NAME" and this browser fills it in. Phone numbers, emails, dates of birth, and record numbers are removed from the details first.

To draw the pictures: the scene for each page, and the look you picked (picture style, age, skin, hair, glasses). Never a photo of your child. Photos you add stay on this phone.

To make the voice: what the friend says on each page. If you choose to hear your child's name, the first name is part of that text. It is used to make the audio and is not saved.

If a parent records their voice: the recording is sent once to ElevenLabs to make a copy of that voice, only after the parent ticks the consent box. The copy stays at ElevenLabs until you tap "Delete my voice" or "Delete everything". Only the voice id is kept on this phone.

For Play Coach: the game picked for today, the age group, the talk level, the favorite thing, and the language. Never the name, and never the play log. The play log stays on this phone.

Play Coach games come from a fixed library built on parent-led, play-based approaches with strong research: PACT (Green et al., Lancet 2010; Pickles et al., Lancet 2016), ESDM (Dawson et al., Pediatrics 2010), JASPER (Kasari et al.), and Hanen "More Than Words". The AI only rewrites a game around the child's favorite thing. It is not therapy and does not replace an evaluation.

To make videos: the page's picture and its scene are sent to fal.ai (Kling video model). The clip is saved on this phone. Videos are only made when you tap "Make it a real video".

"What scares them most" uses a fixed list of ways to help from pediatric and autism care guidance (HELPinKIDS, AAP, American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry, Autism Speaks ATN/AIR-P toolkits, child life programs). The AI picks from that list. It does not search the web, so it cannot pick up advice that is wrong or unsafe.

Everything runs on Cloudflare Workers AI: Llama 3.3 70B writes, Leonardo Lucid Origin draws, Deepgram Aura and MeloTTS speak. Languages without an AI voice use the device's own voice. The server does not save or log anything, and Cloudflare does not use it to train models.

How stories are kept safe

  • Stories follow social story practice: they describe instead of giving orders, are honest about anything that may hurt and for how long, and never say "be brave" or "don't cry".
  • Every story includes the comfort item, a page saying it is OK to feel worried, and a happy ending. If the AI leaves them out, they are added back.
  • Pictures are gentle, with no blood and no needles touching skin.
  • You see every page before your child does, and can redraw any picture, pick a simple one, or use your own photo.

Limits

  • This is not medical advice. Stories describe what usually happens. Your clinic may do things differently.
  • AI pictures are not perfect. Sometimes a sign has nonsense letters or a detail is off. Redraw it or swap it.
